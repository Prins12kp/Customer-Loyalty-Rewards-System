# Product Assumptions

## Product concept
- Northstar Goods is the sample factory brand used in this prototype.
- The reward system is available to individual consumers who buy physical products in boxes.
- The initial prototype is a factory-owner operations dashboard. Customer-facing enrollment and redemption screens are represented through the dashboard workflow but are not implemented as separate authentication screens.
- “Points” are a platform reward balance earned when a valid purchase code is redeemed.
- A 12-pack is 500 points, a 6-pack is 300 points, 10% store credit is 250 points, and free shipping is 150 points as illustrative rewards.

## Purchase proof
- Every saleable box contains a unique, printed QR code and alphanumeric fallback code.
- Customers scan the QR code or enter the fallback code while signed into their customer account.
- A code is accepted only after the platform validates that it exists, is for the customer’s claimed product, has not expired, and has not already been redeemed.
- A product purchase may also be supported by a retailer receipt or uploaded proof for exceptions. Receipt review is assumed to be an administrative fallback, not the normal workflow.
- The current prototype uses local mock data and does not perform real receipt, barcode, or account verification.

## Fraud and code reuse prevention
- Codes are generated by the factory, stored as one-way hashes in production, and signed or encrypted in the print data to prevent easy guessing.
- Every redemption attempt is atomic: only one account can change a code to “redeemed” at a time.
- Redeemed codes cannot earn points again, even if the account or page is retried.
- Duplicate-device, duplicate-address, unusual velocity, bulk redemption, and mismatched geolocation signals place transactions in a review queue rather than automatically destroying legitimate rewards.
- Customers receive clear success, already-redeemed, invalid, expired, and review-required outcomes.
- Administrators can approve, reject, or flag a redemption; blocked codes remain blocked until an authorized audit action occurs.
- No sensitive customer or payment information is required for this prototype.

## Owner management and monitoring
- The factory owner can monitor total rewards issued, active customers, unique codes redeemed, reward cost, recent redemptions, fraud scores, blocked codes, reuse attempts, and review queue items.
- Reward creation is assumed to include a name, points cost, reward type, and description.
- The owner can filter redemption records by search text and status and can export activity reports.
- The owner can approve or reject suspicious activity in the full implementation, while this front-end prototype demonstrates the available status and review interactions.
- Dashboard metrics, customer records, chart values, and redemption records are illustrative seeded data.
- Currency is US dollars and dates use the current month in the visualized chart.

## Technology and scope
- This prototype is a responsive React and TypeScript single-page application running on Vite.
- Interactions are client-side demonstrations; there is no backend, database, authentication server, QR scanner, payment processor, or production audit log.
- In a production implementation, backend APIs, secure sessions, role-based access, encryption, immutable audit logs, rate limiting, idempotency keys, and database transactions would be required.
- Mobile, tablet, and desktop layouts are assumed to be sufficient for launch while native applications remain out of scope.
